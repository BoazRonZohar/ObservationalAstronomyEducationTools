Transit Finder · transit calculator

Exoplanet transit times

Enter your telescope's coordinates and an observing date — the tool works out which exoplanet transits, from a list of bright planets, can be imaged from that place, with start / mid / end times and the altitude above the horizon.

Observing site and date

The calculator uses a set of 107 transiting exoplanets, extracted and processed from TEPCat — a maintained academic catalogue (Southworth, Keele University) devoted to exactly the parameters observation planning needs (position, magnitude, ephemeris). It covers planets down to about magnitude 14.7, not the full 6,000+ known planets (many of them too faint for any equipment). The ephemeris times come from the most recent publication for each system, but may still drift a little. For a final check, cross-reference against the NASA Exoplanet Archive ephemeris service or the Exoplanet Transit Database (ETD) (the new portal, VarAstro).

Ephemeris data: public literature sources (discovery papers / NASA Exoplanet Archive). Times are shown in the local clock you entered and in UTC. The altitude and azimuth are computed with standard astronomical formulae (LST, a low-precision solar position method). The tool does not account for cloud, a local horizon (hills, buildings), or the Moon.
